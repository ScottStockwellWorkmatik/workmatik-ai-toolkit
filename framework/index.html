AI Framework Reference

What domain, capability, skill, workflow, activity and use case each mean, and how the Workmatik AI tools fit together.

The terms at a glance

One word per level, used the same way in every tool. Capabilities and skills describe what can be done. Roles, workflows, activities and use cases describe the work.

TermWhat it isQuestion it answersLevelReal example
DomainA subject area that groups related items. It is a heading, not something that can be scored on its own.Which area is this in?Grouping“Data & privacy” (AI Skills Assessment)
CapabilitySomething a team or function can do reliably, repeatedly and with evidence. It does not depend on one person.Can the function do this?Team or functionG1, “Set AI policy and acceptable use”

An analogy: a restaurant kitchen

  • Domain is the section of the kitchen, such as pastry or grill.
  • Capability is the kitchen being able to put out a perfect dessert at 200 covers, however many cooks are on shift.
  • Skill is one chef’s knife work or piping.
  • Role is the station a cook works.
  • Workflow is a dish’s journey from order ticket through each station to the plate.
  • Activity is one step on that journey, such as plating the sauce.
  • Use case is tonight’s decision to use a new machine at one step, or to redesign the whole dish.

Skills build capabilities, and capabilities make use cases possible. A use case fails when the capability is missing, even if individual skills are strong. A brilliant prompter with no approved data or policy still cannot deliver.

How they nest

Two sides of one organisation. The left asks what it is able to do. The right asks what work it does. Use cases sit where the two meet.

What the organisation can do

Domain or categoryThe heading that groups related items
CapabilityFunction level. Scored in the AI Capability Assessment
SkillIndividual level. Scored in the AI Skills Assessment

What work it does

Role → Task → ActivityWho does what. Sorted in the AI Roles Assessment
Workflow → Step → ActivityHow the work flows end to end

The same activity belongs to one role and sits in one or more workflows. Roles are the vertical slices, workflows the horizontal ones.

Use case = an AI intervention at one activity or step, or a redesign of a whole workflow. It needs capabilities (the function can support it) and skills (people can deliver it).

Work modes

A work mode is the kind of work a person is accountable for at a given moment. One person can work in more than one mode, so a campaign manager might be a Practitioner in one task and an Orchestrator in the next. Work modes are the Workmatik framework, not research findings.

Work modeWhat they doIn the kitchen
StrategistOwns outcomes and makes judgement calls. Sets direction for agents, decides when AI output is good enough and when it needs human intervention, and holds the commercial accountability for the pod’s results.The head chef, who decides the menu and when a dish is good enough to leave the pass
OrchestratorDesigns and manages the agent workflows. Defines the quality parameters agents work within, supervises execution at scale, catches failure modes before they compound, and keeps improving the workflow design as capability matures.The kitchen manager who designs the stations and how orders flow between them
GovernorOwns the human decision gates: brand safety, compliance review, quality assurance on AI-generated content, and escalation when an agent output falls outside acceptable parameters.The person who checks allergens and hygiene before a plate goes out
PractitionerTeams with the agents and uses AI to deliver campaigns, build relationships and drive impact, to delight customers with the organisation’s products and services.The cook who works the station with the new equipment

Mindset is different. A mindset is how someone works across all four modes, such as an innovator’s curiosity. It is not a fifth mode: an innovative Governor and an innovative Practitioner still do different work.

Naming rules

  1. Domain and category mean the same thing. The AI Skills Assessment says domain and the AI Capability Assessment says category. Either is fine, but do not use both in one place.
  2. Activity means a unit of work. In the Journey Map each capability also has an action to build it, such as “Set AI policy and acceptable use”. Call that a capability action, so it is not confused with a job activity.
  3. A flow is a lane, not a workflow. The Journey Map’s two flows are the leader and marketer lanes. Keep “workflow” for the work itself.
  4. A group is a heading, not a level. Role groups (such as Strategy and planning) and workflow groups organise lists in the planner. They are not domains and are not scored.
  5. Capability is about the function, skill is about the person. If it can be done by one person leaving and taking it with them, it is a skill, not a capability.

Best practice

Redesign the workflow before choosing tools. Decompose it to small tasks, classify each as automation, augmentation or human only, then redesign around what AI makes possible rather than automating today’s steps. Derive the skills you need from the redesigned workflow, not from existing job descriptions.
